# Condition cards

Print one card per condition. The facilitator assigns the card order from
[`allocation.md`](allocation.md). Use the seed assigned to that condition; do
not let condition order choose the seed.

## Shared task boundary

Use the exact full task prompt matching the assigned seed. The initial request
asks for three directions, then the author chooses two details and one world
decision after seeing those directions. The model must not treat a generated
direction as an author endorsement, invent a protagonist or ending, or silently
convert an open possibility into canon. The author may reject, revise, combine,
or leave material open.

## A — generic Develop route

Open a nonchapter document in the Write desk and use the current generic
Develop action. The author writes or edits the request and manually sends it.
The facilitator records the actual visible labels after checking the build;
this card does not assume exact label text.

Plain document notes may preserve alternatives and decisions. Do not add a
Workshop alternatives manager or any automatic analysis.

## B — worksheet then generic Develop route

Complete the optional [`worksheet-comparator.md`](worksheet-comparator.md) on
paper or as a local blank copy. Unchecked or blank fields remain
**unspecified**. Before sending, the author checks the final assembled
instruction and may edit it. Then follow exactly condition A's generic
nonchapter-document Develop route. The worksheet is a control condition, not a
production setup requirement.

## C — Develop mode Workshop

Open Develop mode Workshop and begin from the assigned seed. Use the Workshop
surface available in the checked build. The author makes every selection and
manually sends every request. Observe whether the saved alternatives, choices,
open items, and drafting-use boundary are understandable and recoverable.

## Common follow-up demands

Use these in the same order in all conditions. They are grouped into at most
three explicit follow-up requests so the default budget remains one initial
request plus up to three follow-ups.

The ungrouped protocol demands are: develop a competent rival or a
relationship; reject an assumption; revise one selected detail while preserving
another; change a foundational decision; find the alternatives; explain what is
currently chosen; identify what drafting may use; and resume after reopening.
The grouped cards below preserve all of those demands.

### F1 — relationship or rival

> Develop a competent rival or a relationship. Show the alternatives without
> selecting one for the author.

After the response, ask the author to explain which alternative, if any, is
currently chosen. This keeps adoption and rationale author-owned while covering
the protocol's rival/relationship, find-alternatives, and currently-chosen
demands.

### F2 — reject and preserve

> Offer a revision that rejects one assumption, revises one selected detail, and
> preserves another. Leave the final acceptance to the author.

The author may make the request narrower, but the same reject/revise/preserve
task must be offered in every condition.

### F3 — foundational change and resume

> Show the consequences of changing one foundational decision. Leave the
> drafting-use boundary and final acceptance to the author.

After the response, ask the author to identify what drafting may use right now,
then close and reopen this condition and resume from the saved material. The
close/reopen step is an observation task, not another generation request. Do
not send an additional generation request for it. Record whether the author can
tell what is chosen, what is merely possible, and what remains open.

## End-state prompts

Ask the same questions after each condition:

1. Which decision do you knowingly endorse, and why does it belong in this
   story?
2. Which material did you reject or correct?
3. What remains open?
4. What may a later drafting request use?
5. What did you have to navigate, re-prompt, or repair?
